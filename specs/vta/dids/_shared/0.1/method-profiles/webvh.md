# Method profile — `did:webvh` and its `did:scid:vh` form

Covers `DidMethodName` **`webvh`** and **`scid:vh`**. A `did:scid:vh:1` identity is the
same log presented under its self-certifying form (VTI-KEY-160 to VTI-KEY-166), so
everything below applies to both; where they differ it says so.

## 1. Eligibility

Eligible. Against [CONVENTIONS.md §3.2](../CONVENTIONS.md#32-eligibility):

| Criterion | How did:webvh meets it |
|---|---|
| 1. Verifiable history | The log (`did.jsonl`): every entry is signed by a key in its `updateKeys` and chained to its predecessor by `versionId` |
| 2. Commitment to successors, more than one | `nextKeyHashes` is a list; an entry's `updateKeys` must each hash to one of the previous entry's `nextKeyHashes`, and need not use all of them |
| 3. Rotation without change; deactivation | Rotation never changes the SCID; a `portable` log can also move host. `deactivated: true` ends the identity |
| 4. Controller-chosen relationships | The controller writes the whole document; the method places `updateKeys` in no relationship |
| 5. Historic resolution | `versionId` and `versionTime` DID parameters |
| 6. Room for `keyRoles` and `revoked` | The document is the controller's; any member may be added |

## 2. The change authority

The `update` role is the log's `updateKeys` and the commitments in `nextKeyHashes`. What
an entry carries depends on the identity's key assurance level (CONVENTIONS.md §3.4):

| Level | `updateKeys` | `nextKeyHashes` | `state.keyAssurance` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | as the method-specific tasks wrote it — often the one signing key | absent, or the routine successor's hash | absent, or `{ "level": 1 }` |
| 2 | `[R(n)]`, a routine key the VTA holds (derived or generated) | `[hash(R(n+1))]` | `{ "level": 2 }` |
| 3 | `[R(n)]`, a routine key generated by the VTA | `[hash(R(n+1)), hash(K)]`, or with a threshold custodian `[hash(R(n+1)), hash(K1), …, hash(Kn)]` | `{ "level": 3, "recoveryCommitment": "<hash(K)>" }`, or `{ "level": 3, "recoveryCommitment": { "threshold": t, "commitments": ["<hash(K1)>", …] } }` |

At level 3, `keyAssurance.recoveryCommitment` is the recovery key's entry in
`nextKeyHashes`, exactly as written there (the multihash string). Every key-role identity is
created with pre-rotation on, and at level 3 every entry has, from its first:

```json
"updateKeys":    ["<routine key R(n)>"],
"nextKeyHashes": ["<hash(routine successor R(n+1))>", "<hash(recovery key K)>"]
```

with `state.keyAssurance.recoveryCommitment` equal to `hash(K)`. The order of
`nextKeyHashes` carries no meaning; `keyAssurance` is what names the recovery commitment.

- **Every entry moves the update authority** (`updateKeyRotates: true`, always). An entry
  the VTA appends reveals its routine successor as `updateKeys: [R(n+1)]`, is signed by it,
  and commits `[hash(R(n+2)), hash(K)]` — the recovery hash carried forward unchanged
  (CONVENTIONS.md §5.3 item 2). R(n) is then `retired`. Adding an attestation key therefore
  moves the update key too, which is why every mutating task in the family is
  `destructive` and why a consent surface shows `updateKeyRotates`.
- **A recovery entry** sets `updateKeys: [K]`, is signed by K (the custodian's
  `recoveryProof`), and commits `[hash(R'), hash(K')]` with a fresh routine successor
  generated by the VTA and a fresh recovery key K' supplied with its proof of possession.
- **Replacing the recovery commitment** is a recovery entry as above that changes no role
  key — signed by K, committing `[hash(R'), hash(K')]` — when the recovery key authorizes it
  (`keys/rotate` with `useRecoveryKey`). Only where K is lost is it an ordinary entry signed
  by the routine successor, committing `[hash(R(n+2)), hash(K')]`, with the quorum's
  approvals shown to the witness.
- **Raising the level** (`vta/dids/upgrade`) is an ordinary entry signed by the current
  update authority — at level 1, whatever key `updateKeys` names — that sets `updateKeys` to
  a fresh routine key, commits as the new level's row says, and writes `state.keyAssurance`.
  From level 1, if the identity was created without pre-rotation, the entry is signed by the
  level-1 key itself, which is the last thing that key ever authorizes.
- **No threshold**: one valid proof by a key in `updateKeys` authorizes an entry. Witnesses
  (§4 below) are the only second party the method offers.

**The limit (CONVENTIONS.md §1.4).** R(n+1) is in the VTA. A party holding the VTA (T2) can
append an entry that reveals R(n+1) and commits only keys of its own, or lowers the level,
after which K authorizes nothing. did:webvh has no way to require K for a routine entry.
Against loss of the VTA (T4), K always works; against compromise, it works only if a
witness enforces continuity (§4). A level-3 identity **MUST** therefore be witnessed, and a
level-2 identity **SHOULD** be.

**A threshold recovery authority.** With a threshold custodian (CONVENTIONS.md §5.7) the
entry commits every member's key, and a recovery entry sets `updateKeys` to the t members'
keys that authorize it — each hashing to a commitment of the previous entry — and carries a
proof by each of them. The method accepts the entry on any one of those proofs; the witness
(§4) and every VTI verifier require all t (VTI-KEY-215). A threshold **routine** authority is
not offered under this profile: every entry would be a t-member ceremony revealing t
committed keys (VTI-KEY-221).

## 3. Versions

`versionId` and `expectedVersionId` carry the log entry's `versionId`
(`<number>-<entryHash>`). A past document is resolved with the `versionId` or `versionTime`
DID parameter. A `did:scid:vh` identity's versions are its log's, and a resolver applies
VTI-KEY-162's version floor, so an old source cannot serve a version before one already
verified — including one carrying a recovery commitment since replaced.

## 4. Rendering, and witnesses

- Verification methods are `Multikey`. The document's `@context` includes
  `https://www.w3.org/ns/did/v1`, `https://w3id.org/security/multikey/v1` (which defines
  `revoked`) and the VTI key-roles context (CONVENTIONS.md §15).
- `keyRoles` is a top-level member of `state`. A revoked method stays in
  `verificationMethod` with `revoked` (CONVENTIONS.md §9.1).
- A `did:scid:vh` identity's document references its methods under the `did:scid` DID
  (VTI-KEY-161), and so does `keyRoles`.
- `keyAssurance` is a top-level member of `state`, rendered by the VTA from the identity's
  level (CONVENTIONS.md §3.4.2), and never set by a template or by `vta/dids/update`.
- **Witnesses.** The `witness` parameter (`threshold`, `witnesses`) names witnesses whose
  proofs (`did-witness.json`) an entry needs before resolvers accept it. A witness meeting
  CONVENTIONS.md §5.4 reads the entry it is asked to witness and the one before it, and
  refuses to witness the entry where:
  1. `state.keyAssurance.level` (absent: 1) is lower than the previous entry's;
  2. it declares level 3 and `state.keyAssurance.recoveryCommitment` is absent, names a
     commitment that is not an element of the entry's `nextKeyHashes`, names every element
     of it, or names a threshold t with t < 2 or t ≥ the number of commitments; or
  3. the previous entry is at level 3, and the entry's `recoveryCommitment` differs from
     the previous one, unless the entry is a recovery entry — its `updateKeys` is `[K]` with
     `hash(K)` equal to the previous `recoveryCommitment`, or, for a threshold custodian, at
     least t keys each hashing to one of the previous commitments, with a valid proof by
     each in the entry's proof set (VTI-KEY-212, VTI-KEY-215) — or the VTA presents the
     approvals `keys/rotate` recorded for a replacement.

  Every check reads only the log, so a witness needs no state of its own beyond the log, and
  no declaration from the VTA. Checking the approvals of item 3 needs the witness to know
  who may approve, which this profile does not yet say; it is flagged for working-group
  review.

## 5. The cache horizon input

The `ttl` parameter (seconds). Absent, did:webvh's default applies. The horizon is
publication + max(`ttl`, 24 hours) (CONVENTIONS.md §10.1).

## 6. Publication

`PublishedArtifact.name` is `did.jsonl` (the appended entry, `application/jsonl`) and, for
a witnessed identity, `did-witness.json`. Where the VTA publishes through a hosting service
it registered the identity with, `selfPublish` is false.

## 7. Recovery signing

`RecoverySigningRequest.unsignedChange` is the log entry, as JSON, without `proof`;
`proofOptions` is the Data Integrity proof configuration the entry's proof uses:

```json
{
  "type": "DataIntegrityProof",
  "cryptosuite": "eddsa-jcs-2022",
  "verificationMethod": "did:key:<K>#<K>",
  "proofPurpose": "assertionMethod",
  "created": "<instant>"
}
```

`recoveryProof` is the resulting proof object. The VTA appends it to the entry and verifies
the entry as a resolver would before publishing. The custodian's check (`RecoverySigningRequest`)
is concrete here: `hash(K)` is the previous entry's `state.keyAssurance.recoveryCommitment`
and in its `nextKeyHashes`, `updateKeys` is `[K]`, and the entry's `nextKeyHashes` includes
`hash(K')`, named as its `recoveryCommitment`, for the fresh key K' the custodian holds.

For a threshold custodian, each of the t members signs the same entry with its own key: the
`eddsa-jcs-2022` proof input — the hash of the canonical proof configuration naming that
member's key, followed by the hash of the canonical entry — which every member computes
itself from `unsignedChange` and its own `proofOptions` (VTI-KEY-214). The entry's `proof` is
then a proof set of t proofs, and `recoveryProof` carries them all. A member **MAY** add an
`mldsa44-jcs-2024` proof by a second key it holds; the method does not count it, a VTI
verifier's policy may (VTI-KEY-219).

## 8. Method parameters

[`webvh-parameters.schema.json`](webvh-parameters.schema.json): `CreateParameters` (hosting service or
self-publication location, domain, path, `portable`, the presented form, the `did:scid`
alias, witnesses, watchers, `ttlSeconds`) and `UpdateParameters` (witnesses, watchers,
`ttlSeconds`). `portable`, `presentAs` and `scidAlsoKnownAs` are fixed at creation; an
update naming them is refused with `vta/dids:methodParameterUnsupported`.

`preRotationCount` does not exist here: a key-role identity always commits exactly one
routine successor, and at level 3 one recovery key.

## 9. Algorithms

Log entries are signed with `eddsa-jcs-2022`, so `update` keys — routine and recovery, every
member key of a threshold custodian included — are `ed25519`. A post-quantum change authority waits for the method to accept a post-quantum
cryptosuite for entries; the committed hashes keep the unused keys' public halves
unpublished meanwhile (CONVENTIONS.md §14 item 4). Verification methods may use any
algorithm §2.2 allows.
